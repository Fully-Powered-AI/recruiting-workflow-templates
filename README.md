**Quick start**

1. **Turn rough hiring notes into a job description.** Open ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Copy the text below into a new chat and replace the square brackets with your own notes. You need nothing else, and nothing is sent or posted anywhere.

   ```text
   Prepare a draft job description for one role from my notes. Do not post
   or send anything. A person approves every word.

   Hiring manager's notes, as typed: [paste your rough notes: why the role
   is needed now, what the person will do, what good looks like at 90 days
   and at a year, what you think is required]

   Role facts: [title, team, reports to, location, hours, full-time or
   part-time]
   Pay range approved by whoever owns pay: [paste it exactly]
   Our approved company wording and how to apply: [paste it exactly]

   Write: why the role exists, the work, what success looks like, a short
   must-have list (five at most) and the team. Copy pay and company wording
   exactly. Mark any missing item as a gap; never invent it. Add review
   notes listing every requirement you moved or left out and why, any
   phrase you removed, and open questions for me.
   ```

2. Read what comes back. It marks what it checked and what is left for you. What comes out: a draft with review notes:

   ```text
   Why this role exists
   Our operations manager builds the weekly schedule alone, in a
   spreadsheet, on Sunday night, and by Tuesday it is wrong. Since we took
   on a trailer-parts customer in the spring, more jobs have run late:
   on-time delivery was 81% last quarter, against the 95% we promise. We
   need one person who owns the schedule every day. You would be our first
   scheduler.

   The work
   - You will own the weekly and daily schedule for three lines (laser,
     press brake and weld) across two shifts.
   - You will run the 7:15 production meeting.
   - You will chase material shortages with purchasing.
   - You will tell customer service early when a job is going to slip.
   ```

3. Stop there. You will know if this is for you. ## The workflow templates here today

- **[Candidate outreach drafting](candidate-outreach-drafting/)**: For each person a recruiter or founder has already chosen to approach about an approved role: a first message and two follow-ups, each personal line traced to a public work source the recruiter supplied, do-not-contact names and opt-outs honoured, and a reply log. The recruiter sends every message.
- **[Hiring pipeline status report](hiring-pipeline-status-report/)**: Each week your people lead or founder sees every open role on one page: applications at each stage, days open against the target, roles running late, pass-through between stages, which sources lead to interviews, and every blocker with its owner. It counts; it never assesses a candidate.
- **[Interview plan and scorecard](interview-plan-and-scorecard/)**: Before the first interview, the hiring manager and every interviewer share one plan: the criteria the role needs, the same questions in the same order for every candidate, and a blank scorecard each person fills in alone. It rates no one. People make every decision about a candidate.
- **[Interview process review after a hiring round](interview-process-review-after-a-hiring-round/)**: After a hiring round closes, the recruiting lead gets a review of the interview process: questions that gave no usable evidence, competencies nobody covered, inconsistent rubric use, and revisions to approve before the next search. It reviews the process only; no candidate is assessed.
- **[Interview scheduling and candidate confirmation](interview-scheduling-and-candidate-confirmation/)**: For candidates your team has already chosen to interview, the recruiting coordinator gets one scheduling sheet that lines up each candidate's offered times, in their own time zone, with the interviewers' free time, plus a ready confirmation or availability request for each. A person sends every message and invitation.
- **[Job description](job-description/)**: A hiring manager’s rough notes become a job description the right people want to answer: why the role exists, the work itself, and only the must-haves that are real. Your approved pay and employer wording go in untouched, and nothing is posted until the hiring manager and the people lead approve it.
- **[Job posting and sourcing plan](job-posting-and-sourcing-plan/)**: For one approved role, the hiring manager gets a plan for where to post and where to look: every channel on the company's own list ordered by the signal it should give for that kind of role, a week-by-week schedule inside the budget, and what to count on each channel. Nothing is posted or paid until a person approves.
- **[Reference check question plan](reference-check-question-plan/)**: After the interviews, the recruiter gets short reference calls aimed only at what the panel still could not establish, put to the referees the candidate named and agreed to, with a note sheet for each call that keeps the referee's own words. It gives no view on the candidate.
- **[Work sample exercise brief](work-sample-exercise-brief/)**: For one approved role, the hiring manager gets a practical exercise: a bounded task from the role's real work, instructions for the candidate with a time limit and adjustments, and the evidence the panel will look for. The panel judges the work; nothing is rated here.

## How to use one

Open a folder and start with its README. Every folder stands alone, so copy the one you want and leave the rest. The templates are written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Gemini: paste the instruction block and the output format in as custom instructions, or load the folder in a product that reads `SKILL.md` packages.

To take the whole collection, press **Use this template** at the top of this page: it copies every folder into a repository in your own account, yours to change.

In every template a person starts the work, supplies the inputs and decides. The template prepares and checks. Anything that sends, posts, pays or changes a record waits for a person to approve it.

## What SKILL.md is

Each folder holds a file named `SKILL.md`. It is the technical file: the same instructions and output format as the template, in the packaged form that AI products which load instructions from a folder read. It is generated from `TEMPLATE.md`, so the two always agree. The workflow template is the whole folder; `SKILL.md` is one way of loading it. If your AI product does not read that format, use `TEMPLATE.md`.

## What "checked" means here

Each folder says exactly what was checked and when: who wrote it, that its sources and rights were reviewed, the date of its editorial review, and whether it has been tried on its example in a named AI product. Nothing is described as tested for a client unless it was. Test a template on your own past work before your team relies on it; each one comes with acceptance tests for that.
